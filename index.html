Beans, markets and what Australians pay

From October 2025 to September 2026, the ICO’s composite coffee price peaked in November and reached its lowest point in June.

So, where does our coffee come from?

Among the 22 selected supplier countries, Brazil, Colombia and Vietnam supplied more than half of the recorded import weight in 2025, and all three are among the world’s largest producers.

At home, Australia’s cafés and restaurants are mostly small businesses. And while the world bean price fell 12% from October 2025 to August 2026, Australian coffee, tea and cocoa prices fell only 1.5%.

Good to know

  • Cafés and restaurants: the Australian Bureau of Statistics counts them together in one industry class (4511), so the numbers include restaurants. They count businesses, not shop fronts.
  • Imports: the 22 countries are coffee-growing countries chosen from the Food and Agriculture Organization list. The customs code 0901 covers green and roasted coffee. Countries that mainly trade coffee, such as Switzerland, are left out. On the flow map every line ends at the middle of Australia, not at the port where the coffee arrived.
  • Shown as published: every country in the FAO production data is included. Reported output for the Central African Republic and Guinea rises sharply after 2015 (the Central African Republic from 9,050 tonnes in 2015 to 289,283 tonnes in 2020); these are FAO’s published figures and are shown unchanged.
  • Coffee years: the International Coffee Organization counts a coffee year from 1 October. Its figures are not the same as the calendar-year figures from the Food and Agriculture Organization. The Organization revises past years: the 2024/25 supply and demand figures here are from its September 2026 report and differ from its June 2026 report. The 2025/26 figures are estimates.
  • Prices: charts 10 to 12 describe the world market. They are not the price of a cup at an Australian café.
  • Comparing world and Australian prices (chart 13): the two measures are not the same kind of number. The world figure is what a pound of green beans sold for; the Australian figures are price indexes for what households pay. They are put on the same scale by setting all three to 100 in October 2025, which shows the direction and size of each move, not the level. The Australian Bureau of Statistics measures “coffee, tea and cocoa” as one expenditure class and “meals out and take away foods” as another, so neither is the price of a cup of coffee on its own. The window is the 11 months where both sources overlap.
  • The 51% figure comes from a Roy Morgan survey of Australians aged 14 and over. It appears in a release about news readership, where coffee is used as a comparison. No other free, recent survey on coffee drinking in Australia was found.
